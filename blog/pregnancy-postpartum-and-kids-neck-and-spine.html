Pregnancy, Postpartum and Kids: Gentle Chiropractic Care for the Whole Family

By Dr. Sylvia Frierson, DC · October 30, 2026

Families go through a lot of physical change: pregnancy, carrying a newborn, toddlers who climb everything, kids who fall off bikes. Here is a plain-language look at how the body changes during these seasons and what gentle chiropractic care looks like for the whole family.

Education only. This article is general information, not a diagnosis or medical advice. Always follow the guidance of your OB, midwife or pediatrician. Severe pain, bleeding, fever, a head injury, or anything that worries you needs emergency care right away.

How pregnancy changes posture

During pregnancy the body shifts in many ways. The center of gravity moves forward as the belly grows, and hormones soften ligaments to prepare for birth. Many people find their lower back curves more, their shoulders roll forward, and their upper back and neck work harder to keep the head balanced.

Sleep also changes. Side-sleeping with pillows is common, and finding a comfortable neck position can take some experimenting.

The postpartum season

After delivery, the body keeps changing, and the daily demands of caring for a baby add up. Think of how many hours are spent hunched over feeding, rocking, lifting car seats and bending over a crib.

Some small habits may help:

  • Bring the baby to you. Use a pillow to raise the baby to chest height instead of leaning forward.
  • Support your back and arms. Sit with your back supported and your feet flat.
  • Switch sides. Alternate which arm you carry with, and which side you lean on.
  • Look up and out. Between feedings, take a moment to lift your gaze and roll your shoulders.

Be patient with yourself. Your body has done something big.

Kids and their spines

Children are active. Falls from playground equipment, tumbles, sports and even birth itself are all part of how little bodies learn to move. Most kids bounce back from everyday bumps, and most need nothing more than a hug and a snack.

School-age kids also spend time with backpacks, tablets and phones, which can encourage a forward head position. Gentle reminders to take movement breaks, carry a backpack on both shoulders and keep screens at eye level can help.

If your child has had a significant fall or a head injury, see their pediatrician first.

What gentle care looks like

Dr. Sylvia is an upper cervical chiropractor, which means her focus is the top two bones of the neck where the skull meets the spine. Her approach is instrument-assisted. There is no twisting, no cracking and no popping, which is one reason families often feel comfortable with it. She can adjust her approach and pressure to fit the person in front of her, whether that is a parent, a pregnant mom or a child.

Every family is different, so there is no one-size-fits-all plan. Dr. Sylvia explains each step before it happens, and you are always welcome to ask questions or pause.

Visits happen at your home in Greenville, Spartanburg or Gaffney, which can make a real difference when you have a new baby, a nap schedule or a house full of kids.

Questions to bring with you

Whatever stage your family is in, it helps to write down what you have noticed: when the discomfort shows up, what position seems to make it worse, and any recent falls or injuries. If you are pregnant or have a little one, let your care team know and check with your OB, midwife or pediatrician as well.

No one can promise what will happen for any one person. If you are curious whether upper cervical care is a fit for your family, ask about an evaluation.
