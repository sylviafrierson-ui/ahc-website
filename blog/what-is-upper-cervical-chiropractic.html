What Is Upper Cervical Chiropractic? How It Works in Plain Language

By Dr. Sylvia Frierson, DC · November 2, 2026

You may have heard the term “upper cervical chiropractic” and wondered how it differs from other kinds of chiropractic care. Here is a plain-language overview of what it is, how it is approached, and what a visit can look like.

Education only. This article is general information, not a diagnosis or medical advice. Sudden, severe pain, or symptoms such as fever, a stiff neck, confusion, weakness, trouble speaking or vision changes, or a recent hit to the head, need emergency care right away.

The upper neck, in simple terms

“Cervical” means the neck, and “upper cervical” refers to the top two bones, the atlas (C1) and the axis (C2). They sit right where your skull meets your spine and are close to the brainstem, which connects your brain with the rest of your body.

These bones are very mobile, since they let you nod, tilt and turn your head. Because they move so freely, they rely heavily on small muscles and ligaments to stay steady.

A focused, specific approach

Upper cervical practitioners concentrate on just this region instead of adjusting the whole spine. The idea is precision: find out how the top of your neck is positioned, and tailor care to that, rather than using the same routine for everyone.

Different upper cervical techniques exist. Dr. Sylvia uses instrument-assisted methods, including Orthospinology and the Activator Method, which are gentle and do not involve twisting or popping the neck.

What an evaluation can include

Every provider works a little differently, but an upper cervical evaluation often includes:

  1. Your story. Your history, your goals, past injuries such as falls, sports or car accidents, and what you have noticed day to day.
  2. A look at posture and movement. How your head sits, how your shoulders line up, and how your neck moves side to side.
  3. Imaging, if appropriate. Dr. Sylvia usually recommends 3D imaging to see exactly where the top two bones sit. She explains it to you first, and it is always your choice.

Why a house call?

Dr. Sylvia brings care to your home across Greenville, Spartanburg, Gaffney and Shelby. Many people find that easier when they are caring for little ones, recovering from a busy season or simply short on time. You stay in a familiar space, and there is no waiting room.

What a visit can feel like

Many people expect a chiropractic visit to involve twisting or cracking. With instrument-assisted methods, the experience is different. Dr. Sylvia uses a small handheld instrument to deliver a light, precise input at the spot she has identified, while you sit or lie comfortably.

Afterward she may suggest simple things to notice over the following days and will check in on how you are doing. The pace and number of visits depend on you and on what she finds, and she will explain her thinking before you decide anything.

Who asks about it

People look into upper cervical care for many reasons: a past fall or car accident, long desk days, a new baby, sleep position, or simply curiosity about how their neck works. Families sometimes ask about care for children too, and gentle methods are chosen with age and comfort in mind.

What it is not

Upper cervical care is not a replacement for medical care, and chiropractic does not diagnose or treat diseases. It focuses on how the spine and its supporting tissues are functioning. If something needs a medical evaluation, Dr. Sylvia will tell you so.

Responses vary from person to person, and no one can promise a particular outcome.

Questions to ask any provider

Whoever you choose, it is reasonable to ask what the evaluation involves, why each step is recommended, what the care feels like, and how progress is checked over time. A good provider will answer in plain language and respect your decisions.

If you are curious whether upper cervical care is a fit for you, ask about an evaluation.
