Sleep, Posture, and Your Neck: Simple Habits for Better Rest

By Dr. Sylvia Frierson, DC · October 30, 2026

You spend about a third of your life asleep, and your neck is along for the ride the whole time. Small changes in how you sleep and how you sit during the day can make your neck feel more comfortable. Here are the basics Dr. Sylvia shares in her community talks.

Education only. This article is general information, not medical advice. If you have neck pain that is severe, follows an injury, or comes with numbness, weakness or headaches that are new or getting worse, see a healthcare provider.

How your sleep position affects your neck

The goal in any position is the same: keep your head and neck roughly in line with the rest of your spine, the way they are when you stand tall.

  • Back sleepers: a pillow that supports the curve of your neck without pushing your head forward. Many people do well with a thinner pillow and a small roll under the neck.
  • Side sleepers: a pillow thick enough to fill the space between your ear and the mattress, so your head does not tip down toward the bed or up toward the ceiling. A pillow between the knees helps keep the hips level too.
  • Stomach sleepers: this position keeps your head turned to one side for hours, which is hard on the top of the neck. If you can, try starting the night on your side.

Choosing the right pillow

There is no single best pillow. The right one depends on how you sleep and the width of your shoulders. A quick check: lie in your usual position and have someone look at you from the side (or take a photo). Your nose should line up with the middle of your chest, not tipped up or down. If your pillow has gone flat or lumpy, it is probably time for a new one.

Easy posture habits for everyday life

What you do during the day shows up at night. Your head weighs about as much as a bowling ball, and every inch it drifts forward adds load to the muscles at the base of your skull.

  • Raise your screens. Bring your phone and laptop up toward eye level instead of dropping your head down to them.
  • Take movement breaks. Every 30 to 45 minutes, stand up, roll your shoulders and gently look side to side.
  • Long drives: sit back so your head rests near the headrest, and stop for a stretch every hour or two.
  • Carrying little ones: switch sides often and hold your child close to your body instead of on one hip all day.

When to have your neck looked at

If your neck is stiff most mornings, one side always feels tighter, or you notice your head tilting when you look straight ahead, it is worth asking about an evaluation. Dr. Sylvia brings gentle, instrument-assisted upper cervical care to your home, with no twisting or popping.
