Dr. Sylvia’s Favorites

The Things I Actually Use at Home

Dr. Sylvia Frierson, DC · At Home Chiropractic

Patients ask me all the time what pillow I sleep on, which deodorant I use, or what I cook with. So I put it all in one place.

I try to keep the things that touch my skin and my food every day as simple and natural as I can. I make a lot of my own products, and I’ve shared those recipes at the bottom of this page. Everything else here is something I’ve bought, used and kept using.

None of this replaces your care plan, and nothing here is meant to diagnose or treat anything. It’s just what works for me. If you’re not sure something is a fit for you, ask me at your next visit.

My DIY recipes

I make as many of my own products as I can. Most of them start with beef tallow, so that’s where I’ll start too.

My secret weapon: AI as a soap calculator

I never cut my fat to match a recipe. Instead, I weigh however much tallow I rendered and ask an AI assistant to build the recipe around that exact amount, so I use every bit of it. It works great as a soap calculator. Here’s a prompt you can copy and paste:

I’m making cold process bar soap. I have ___ grams of rendered beef tallow. Calculate how much sodium hydroxide (NaOH) lye and distilled water I need for a 5% superfat and about a 33% lye concentration. Also tell me a safe amount of these essential oils: ___. Give me step-by-step instructions with temperatures, and show your math.

Always double-check the lye. AI can make math mistakes, and too much lye makes soap that burns skin. Before you mix anything, put your numbers into a free lye calculator app like Simple SoapCalc, Saponora or Soaper. If the lye amounts don’t match, don’t make the batch.

Step 1: Rendering beef tallow (the hardest part)

I get my beef fat for free from a butcher. Ask yours. Many are happy to give it away.

  1. Grind it. Smaller pieces give you more tallow. Grind the fat while it’s still frozen in chunks, or the grinder clogs.
  2. Render it low and slow. Put the ground fat in an Instant Pot with the lid off, on low. I don’t add water or salt. That only adds an extra step.
  3. Take your time. Mine often renders on and off over several days, because I start on a weekend and then work gets busy. That’s fine.
  4. Strain it. Pour it through cheesecloth while it’s hot. Wear insulated rubber gloves so you can twist and squeeze the cloth. That gets a lot more of the liquid fat through.
  5. Clean it 2–3 times. Let it cool and harden, then scrape the impurities off the bottom. Remelt and repeat until the bottom comes out clean.
  6. Store it airtight in the fridge or freezer until you’re ready to make soap or lotion.

Hot fat causes serious burns. Keep kids and pets away while you strain it.

Step 2: Beef tallow hard soap

Here’s an example batch for 1,000 g of tallow. Your amounts will change with however much tallow you have, so use the AI prompt above and double-check the lye.

Lye safety first: wear goggles and gloves, work with good ventilation, and keep kids and pets out of the room. Always add the lye to the water, never water to lye.

Example batch

  • 1,000 g rendered beef tallow
  • 136 g sodium hydroxide (NaOH) lye, 5% superfat
  • 276 g distilled water
  • Up to 30 g essential oils (optional)

Steps

  1. Slowly add the lye to the distilled water and stir until dissolved. It gets very hot. Let it cool to about 100–120°F.
  2. Melt the tallow and let it cool to about 100–120°F. A laser thermometer makes this easy.
  3. Pour the lye water into the tallow. Use the immersion blender in short bursts, stirring in between, until it reaches “trace” (it thickens like pudding and leaves a trail on top).
  4. Stir in your essential oils.
  5. Pour into molds, cover, and leave it undisturbed for 24–48 hours.
  6. Unmold, cut into bars, and let them cure on a rack for 4–6 weeks before using.

Whipped beef tallow lotion

My everyday moisturizer. This is the soft version, about 70% tallow and 30% jojoba.

Ingredients

  • 140 g (about ⅔ cup) rendered beef tallow (how I render it is above)
  • 60 g (about ⅓ cup) jojoba oil
  • 4 mL frankincense essential oil
  • 2 mL copaiba essential oil
  • 1 mL each lavender, Roman chamomile, geranium and ylang ylang essential oil

Steps

  1. Melt the tallow slowly.
  2. Let it cool to 95–110°F (35–43°C), then stir in the jojoba oil and essential oils.
  3. Once it cools to 75–85°F, start whipping.
  4. Whip until soft and opaque, with the edges firming up and the center still a little soft, like pudding or softened butter.

The essential oils are optional. If your skin is sensitive, try a small patch first.

Olive oil liquid soap

Lye safety first: wear gloves and eye protection, work with good ventilation, and keep kids and pets out of the room. Always add the lye to the water, never water to lye. Weigh everything on a kitchen scale.

Ingredients

  • 345 g extra-virgin olive oil
  • 71.4 g potassium hydroxide (KOH), 90% pure. This is the lye for liquid soap; sodium hydroxide won’t work.
  • 214 g distilled water

Steps

  1. Slowly add the KOH to the distilled water and stir until dissolved.
  2. Warm the olive oil, then pour in the lye solution.
  3. Alternate 10–20 second bursts of stick blending with stirring until a thick paste forms.
  4. Cook on low for about 3–4 hours, stirring every 30 minutes, until it’s glossy and translucent with no separated oil.
  5. Check that it’s done: a small piece should dissolve clear in hot water.
  6. Dilute the paste with hot distilled water, adding a little at a time until it’s as thin as you like, and let it sit overnight, stirring now and then.

If your KOH isn’t 90% pure, run the recipe through a soap calculator before making it.
